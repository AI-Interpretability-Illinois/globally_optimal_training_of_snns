1 Introduction
[18] described the first mathematical model of a neuron as a threshold activation function. Early
primitives in Artificial Intelligence built on this idea, the most notable example being the per-
ceptron, which used a Heaviside step function as its non-linearity [3]. However, the inherent
non-differentiability and discontinuity of threshold activations made these models difficult to train.
As a result, most of the community gradually shifted toward smooth or piecewise-smooth activations
such as sigmoid [19], tanh , and eventually ReLU [1], all of which provided desirable analytical
properties while loosely approximating the behaviour of biological neurons.
After the formulation and widespread adoption of Back Propogation [23], the fields of Artificial
Intelligence and Neuroscience progressed almost independently for several decades. This separation
persisted until [14] reintroduced spiking neurons into modern AI. Yet, despite their biological
relevance, the non-differentiability and discrete event-driven nature of spiking neurons made them
incompatible with standard gradient-based optimization, and numerous heuristic training method-
ologies were proposed [20, 6]. In parallel, the rise of LLMs and large-scale models brought enormous
training energy costs [2] and a gradual erosion of interpretability [15], creating bottlenecks in the
development of reliable and resource-efficient AI systems. These challenges have renewed interest in
bio-plausible AI [5], inspiring new benchmarks , training paradigms [20, 14, 16], and applications in
robotics [?].
However,acentralproblemremainsunresolved: thedominanttrainingalgorithmsforbio-inspired
models rely on approximations of the true activation functions. Surrogate gradients smooth the
threshold or spike non-linearity to enable backpropagation [20], and ANN–SNN conversion pipelines
train a continuous network before mapping it onto a discrete one [6]. Both approaches introduce
discrepancies between the trained and deployed models, which may contribute to the comparatively
poorer performance of bio-inspired networks on conventional benchmarks [22, 28, 29]. Furthermore,
recent studies indicate that ANN–SNN conversion does not reliably reduce training energy costs
and may only offer inference-time improvements. However, most contemporary work in this area,
including LLM-like architectures [31, 27], ResNet-inspired SNNs [22], and novel designs such as
Continuous Thought Machines [4],continues to rely on these approximation-based training schemes,
without guaranty of optimality.
In parallel, recent work on convexifying ReLU-based networks using path-regularization has
shown that several feedforward architectures admit zero-duality gap formulations under parallel
parametrizations [10, 26, 12]. These results are exact and do not rely on approximations of the
activation. Thisraisesanaturalquestion: whetherananalogousconvexstructureexistsforthreshold
1

activations, which exhibit the same non-differentiability that motivates surrogate methods. The
goal of this paper is to extend the path-regularized framework to parallel feedforward and recurrent
threshold networks and to show that these models also satisfy zero-duality gap under suitable
assumptions. We choose to study Threshold Activations before moving on to Spiking dynamics
since they are easier to formalise and behave in a similar manner to the spiking neurons.
Related Work. Our work is an extension of a long line of research on convexifying neural models,
beginningwith[10]andsubsequentlyexpandedtoCNNs[11], Transformers[8], neuralnetworkbased
generative models [30], and a general framework for feedforward parallel networks [13]. The core
foundations of our approach build on [24], which introduced the path-regularizer; [21], which applied
this regularizer to train vanilla ReLU RNNs; and [9], which established global optimality guarantees
for path-regularized feedforward parallel ReLU networks and proposed an improved algorithm that
accelerates the original procedure of [7] for training parallel feedforward threshold networks to
global optimality. Except for [9], all prior works rely on standard weight-decay regularization during
training. In the bio-inspired side of research , [17] recently showed that all QP and LP problems
can be cast as an shallow-SNN training problem. They do not show that we can go in the opposite
direction for deep-SNNs.
Contributions. In our work, we extend the [24] framework of path-regularizers to threshold
activationsforallcomponentsexcepttheirnorm-basedcapacitymeasures, sincethresholdactivations
are not Lipschitz. We nonetheless outline possible relaxations that yield an analogous notion of
capacity. Building on the [9] framework for training path-regularized models, we show that parallel
feedforward threshold networks admit a zero-duality gap. We then construct an analogous time-
unrolled formulation for parallel RNN threshold networks and demonstrate that they also exhibit a
zero-duality gap.

2.3 Capacity and Lipschitz Relaxations
Before we move to specific architectures, we note that for threshold networks it is not possible to
obtain a norm-based capacity bound, since the threshold function is not Lipschitz continuous and
hence the Rademacher complexity depends purely on the number of parameters. Refer to [25] for a
detailed discussion on how the number of parameters affects capacity.
We can derive norm-based capacity bounds if we relax threshold functions to be Lipschitz
continuous; several relaxations exist (e.g., using two ReLU clippings, sigmoid functions, or linear
relaxations plus clipping). All of these allow us to use the contraction lemma, which yields a
recursive definition of complexity, similar to the argument in [24].

We briefly outline an experimental setup to evaluate the 3-layer, 2-timestep parallel recurrent
threshold network and the proposed convex formulation.
Baselines. We compare the following training strategies:
• Nonconvex-STE: a standard recurrent network with hard threshold activations trained by
backpropagation and a straight-through estimator (STE) for the non-differentiable σ .

Datasets with T = 2. We propose simple sequence classification tasks with exactly two timesteps:
1. Two-stepXORsequence. Generatepairs(X1,X2) ∈ R2whereeachtimestepisa2-dimensional
binary vector and the label depends on a temporal XOR pattern (e.g., Y = 1 iff exactly one of
the two timesteps satisfies a given linear predicate). This tests the ability to exploit temporal
memory.
2. Rotated MNIST pairs. Sample two consecutive frames per example: X1 is an MNIST digit,
X2 is the same digit rotated by a small random angle. The label is the digit class. Here T = 2
and the recurrence must integrate information from both frames.
3. Moving Gaussian blobs. Generate 2D points drawn from one of two Gaussian clusters at
t = 1 and let the cluster centers move slightly at t = 2. The label is the identity of the generating
cluster. This tests robustness to small temporal deformations.
